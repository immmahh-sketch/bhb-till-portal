# Things to confirm before relying on each module

Every `CHECK` below was raised while turning the paper 10-minute trainers into modules: something the trainer says that may be out of date, or a venue fact to confirm. The module teaches what the trainer says unless it is clearly wrong; change the wording in the module (or the trainer) once you have decided.


## 🔥 Fire Safety Awareness (BHB-10MT-01)
- [ ] assembly point (car park at the bottom of the Orangery) is taken from the trainer as a venue fact; please confirm it is current.
- [ ] the trainer says to call 999 as well as raising the alarm. Many fire procedures have the alarm calling the Fire Service automatically or a manager making the call; confirm what your fire procedure says about who dials 999.

## 🥜 Allergen Awareness (BHB-10MT-02)
- [ ] the trainer says allergen orders should be prepared separately. Current Food Standards Agency guidance supports this; confirm it matches the venue's allergen procedure.

## 🪪 Challenge 25 & Age-Restricted Sales (BHB-10MT-03)
- [ ] the trainer says "the person who makes the sale can be personally fined". This is right, but the law does not set a fixed small fine; please confirm you are happy with the wording used on the slides.
- [ ] the trainer lists photo driving licence, passport and PASS hologram card as valid ID. Some premises also accept military ID or approved digital ID; confirm the venue's policy and update if needed.
- [ ] the refusals log is good practice and usually part of the venue's age verification policy rather than a direct legal requirement; confirm.

## 🛢️ Manual Handling — Moving Kegs & Casks (BHB-10MT-04)
- [ ] the trainer says musculoskeletal injuries account for "roughly a third" of workplace ill-health "across the UK". HSE figures for work-related ill-health have put musculoskeletal disorders at around a quarter to a third in recent years; please confirm the current figure before relying on it. The slides say "about a third", as the trainer does.
- [ ] the trainer says a full 50-litre keg "can weigh over 70kg". A full 50-litre keg is commonly nearer 60 to 65kg including the keg itself, depending on the keg. Please weigh a full one and confirm; the safe message (it is too heavy to throw around) does not change.

## 🔪 Safe Knife Use (BHB-10MT-05)
- [ ] none found, but please confirm the venue's own rules on who sharpens knives and how (the trainer only says a sharp knife is safer).

## ⚠️ Slips, Trips & Falls (BHB-10MT-06)
- [ ] the trainer says slips, trips and falls make up "around 30%" of workplace injuries in hospitality, are the most common cause of major injuries, lead to "hundreds of thousands" of lost working days and cost businesses "more than £500 million" a year. These are HSE-style headline figures but may be dated and are national rather than hospitality-specific; please confirm against current HSE statistics. The slides repeat them as the trainer states them.

## 📝 Accident & Near-Miss Reporting (BHB-10MT-07)
- [ ] the trainer says to write up the accident book "before the end of your shift". That is the venue's own rule rather than a legal deadline; please confirm it is what you want. RIDDOR reporting (to HSE) is the manager's job and is not taught in this trainer.
- [ ] "behind most serious accidents there were usually lots of near-misses" echoes the well-known safety pyramid, which is a rule of thumb rather than a proven law; the slide softens it to "often".

## 👀 Hazard Spotting (BHB-10MT-08)
- [ ] none found. The trainer says to take a faulty appliance "out of use if you can"; confirm the venue's own method for doing this.

## 🧴 COSHH — Safe Use of Chemicals (BHB-10MT-09)
- [ ] the trainer's emergency steps are brief (rinse, get first aid, report). For splashes the product's safety data sheet gives the exact first aid; confirm the venue's procedure matches.

## 🧼 Cross-Contamination Prevention (BHB-10MT-10)
- [ ] colour-coding schemes vary. The trainer gives red for raw meat and green for salad and fruit "and so on". Many kitchens also use blue for raw fish, yellow for cooked meat, brown for vegetables and white for dairy and bakery; confirm the venue's scheme.

## 🧼 Personal Hygiene & Handwashing (BHB-10MT-11)
- [ ] the 20 second wash time is common guidance rather than a figure in the regulation; confirm it matches the venue food safety management system.
- [ ] the 48 hours symptom-free rule matches Food Standards Agency guidance for vomiting and diarrhoea; confirm the venue policy says the same, and what applies to other illnesses.

## 🔒 GDPR & Data Protection (BHB-10MT-12)
- [ ] the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amended parts of UK GDPR (including how requests for information are handled); the trainer does not mention it. Confirm nothing here is out of date.
- [ ] the trainer says to report a breach to the General Manager. Confirm the venue procedure also covers telling the Information Commissioner's Office where required; no time limit is stated in the session.
- [ ] the trainer says a breach can lead to a large fine. Kept as a general statement with no figure.

## 🚫 Spotting Drug Use in Customers (BHB-10MT-13)
- [ ] the trainer says to refuse alcohol to someone under the influence of drugs. Alcohol must not be sold to a drunk person (Licensing Act 2003, s.141); confirm the venue policy for someone you suspect is on drugs but not drunk.
- [ ] the signs listed (frequent toilet trips, nosebleeds, jitteriness) can have innocent causes, including medical ones. The slides say they are signs to pass to a manager, not proof.

## 🪜 Ladder & Step-Ladder Safety (BHB-10MT-16)
- [ ] the trainer says to have a colleague "foot" the ladder for higher or awkward tasks. HSE guidance prefers to secure (tie) a ladder, and footing is a fallback where tying is not possible; confirm the venue wording.
- [ ] the trainer says ladders suit "short, light jobs". HSE guidance describes ladders as low-risk, short-duration equipment, with a duration given in guidance; no figure is quoted here, so confirm what the venue wants stated.
- [ ] "surprising number of serious workplace falls" is the trainer's claim and no statistic is repeated.

## 🔌 Visual Checks of Electrical Items (BHB-10MT-17)
- [ ] the trainer teaches user visual checks only. The session does not mention formal inspection or portable appliance testing, which is the venue's decision on risk; confirm the venue policy.

## 🌡️ Temperature Control — Cook, Chill, Hold (BHB-10MT-19)
- [ ] the trainer gives the danger zone as roughly 8°C to 63°C. FSA guidance describes it as roughly 5°C to 63°C. The 8°C figure is the legal maximum for chilled food. Kept as the trainer says.
- [ ] the trainer gives 75°C as a "common target" core temperature. FSA guidance describes 70°C for 2 minutes (or equivalent) as the standard; 75°C is a common venue target. Confirm the venue figure.
- [ ] cooling "ideally within about 90 minutes" is industry guidance rather than a legal figure; confirm it matches the venue procedure.
- [ ] hot food held at 63°C or above has limited legal exceptions (for example, short service periods); none are taught here.

## ❄️ Fridge & Freezer / Cold Chain Checks (BHB-10MT-20)
- [ ] the trainer says fridges run at 5°C or below. The legal maximum for chilled food is 8°C, and 5°C is the usual venue target (as in the temperature control session); confirm the venue figure.
- [ ] freezers at -18°C or below is standard good practice for frozen food rather than a general legal figure; confirm the venue figure.

## 🗣️ Complaint Handling (BHB-10MT-21)
- [ ] the trainer says research "consistently shows" a well-handled complaint can leave a guest more loyal. This is a widely quoted service-recovery finding, but the trainer gives no source, and the slides state it without citing a study.
- [ ] allergic reactions, illness and injury must go to the Duty Manager; confirm the venue also expects these to be recorded (accident book, allergen incident log) after the guest has been looked after.

## 🛡️ Spotting Child Sexual Exploitation (CSE) (BHB-10MT-23)
- [ ] the trainer sends every concern to the Duty Manager, who decides what happens next. Confirm the venue has a named safeguarding lead and a clear route to the police (101) and children's services, so the Duty Manager is not left to decide alone.
- [ ] the trainer lists warning signs only in outline (adult booking a room with an anxious or controlled young person, secrecy, little control over money, phone or movements). Confirm they match current guidance, such as that from the police and the NSPCC, before relying on the list.
- [ ] the trainer says to keep concerns confidential. Confirm the venue also tells staff that confidentiality never stops a safeguarding report being passed to the Duty Manager or the police.

## 🤝 Bullying & Harassment (BHB-10MT-24)
- [ ] bullying has no single legal definition in England; the trainer's wording ("offensive, intimidating, malicious or humiliating") follows the ACAS description. Harassment under the Equality Act must relate to a protected characteristic, so the trainer's general wording is wider than the legal test.
- [ ] the trainer says bullying and harassment have no place "even from a guest". Employer liability for harassment by guests and other third parties has been changing under the Employment Rights Act 2025; confirm what applies in October 2026 and that the venue's policy matches.
- [ ] the trainer says concerns are handled confidentially and without anyone being treated badly. Confirm the venue's grievance procedure and any anti-victimisation wording are written down and match the ACAS Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures.

## 🚫 Sexual Harassment in the Workplace (BHB-10MT-25)
- [ ] the trainer says employers must take "reasonable steps" to prevent sexual harassment, including by guests. From October 2026 the Employment Rights Act 2025 is expected to raise this to "all reasonable steps" and to strengthen protection for harassment by third parties. Confirm the wording and the date before the session is relied on.
- [ ] the trainer says no one will be victimised for raising a genuine concern (Equality Act 2010, section 27). Confirm the venue has a written policy, a named person to report to and a record of the steps it takes.

## 🌍 Equality & Diversity (BHB-10MT-26)
- [ ] the trainer's definition of indirect discrimination ("a rule or practice that unfairly disadvantages a particular group") is simplified. In law it is a provision, criterion or practice that puts people sharing a protected characteristic at a particular disadvantage, and that the employer or provider cannot justify. Confirm the simplified wording is acceptable for staff.

## 🍺 The Perfect Serve (Bar) (BHB-10MT-27)
- [ ] the trainer says measuring spirits correctly is a legal requirement. It is for spirits sold by the measure (the measure sizes sold must be displayed and prescribed quantities used for some spirits), but not every drink or every cocktail is covered. Confirm the venue's measures and notices, and that wine is offered in the required sizes.
- [ ] the trainer treats a chipped glass as a safety issue to be binned. Confirm the venue's broken and chipped glass procedure matches.

## 🛢️ Cellar Safety & CO2 Awareness (BHB-10MT-28)
- [ ] the trainer does not say the venue's alarm must be tested and maintained, or that cylinder changes need trained, authorised staff. Confirm the venue's CO2 detector is installed, tested and serviced on a schedule, and who is authorised to change cylinders.
- [ ] the trainer treats "if the alarm sounds, do not go in" as the rule. Confirm the venue has a written emergency procedure for a collapsed person in the cellar (call 999, do not enter to rescue) and that staff have been told it.

## 🪜 Working at Height (BHB-10MT-29)
- [ ] the trainer says falls from height are the single biggest cause of workplace deaths in the UK, and cause thousands of serious injuries. HSE statistics have long listed falls from height as the biggest cause of fatal workplace injuries; confirm the latest HSE annual figures before this wording is relied on.
- [ ] the trainer is for maintenance and housekeeping staff. Confirm the venue has rules on who may use towers and platforms, and on who inspects ladders and steps.

## 💡 Minor Maintenance: Changing Lightbulbs (BHB-10MT-30)
- [ ] the trainer says some bulbs, such as fluorescent tubes, need special disposal. Confirm which types the venue uses and the correct waste route (for example separate collection for fluorescent tubes).
- [ ] the trainer says to isolate the light "where you can". Confirm who is competent to isolate a circuit at the venue, and that staff are not expected to touch wiring or open fittings beyond swapping a bulb.

## 🔥 Preventing Burns & Scalds (BHB-10MT-31)
- [ ] the trainer says cool a burn under cool running water for at least 20 minutes. This matches current NHS and St John Ambulance first aid advice, but please confirm it matches your first aid provider's training.

## 💧 Wet Floor & Spillage Management (BHB-10MT-32)
- [ ] the trainer says slips are "the most common accident in our industry". HSE reports slips and trips as the most common cause of major injury across all workplaces, but the hospitality claim is not verified here. The slides say "one of the biggest causes" only.

## 📞 Bomb Threat & Suspicious Packages (BHB-10MT-33)
- [ ] Martyn's Law (the Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025) places new duties on venues, depending on capacity, with a phased start. Please confirm whether and when it applies to the venue and whether this trainer needs updating.
- [ ] police advice on suspicious items is often summarised as HOT (Hidden, Obviously suspicious, Typical). The trainer's "do not touch" and alert-the-Duty-Manager approach is taught as written; confirm it matches current counter-terrorism guidance.

## 🏃 Security & Lockdown — Run, Hide, Tell (BHB-10MT-34)
- [ ] Run, Hide, Tell is the national police and counter-terrorism advice and is taught as the trainer states it. Please confirm it is still the current wording.
- [ ] Martyn's Law (Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025) may place duties on the venue depending on capacity. Confirm whether and when it applies.

## 🩹 First Aid Awareness (BHB-10MT-35)

## 🚿 Legionella & Water Hygiene Awareness (BHB-10MT-54)

- [ ] the trainer gives roughly 20°C to 45°C as the growth range, which matches HSE; confirm the venue wording.
- [ ] the trainer says people catch it by breathing in droplets, not by drinking the water. HSE notes that inhaling droplets is the usual route; confirm the wording is acceptable.
