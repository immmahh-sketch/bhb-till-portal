# Team Training: facts only the venue can fill in

Each session says "your manager will show you" for these. Agree the answers, then brief managers so they can show new starters. Lists are from each session's `siteNotes`; the legal points to confirm are at the end.


## 🔥 Fire safety and evacuation

- [ ] Where the assembly point is, and the back-up if it is blocked
- [ ] Where the fire alarm panel is and how to read it
- [ ] Where the manual call points and fire extinguishers are, and which types are fitted
- [ ] The venue's evacuation plan, including bedrooms, the restaurant, the bar and the marquee or function room
- [ ] Who the fire marshals are and who does the roll call
- [ ] How each department finds out who is on shift, and how guests who need help are recorded
- [ ] Fire door locations and which doors must never be propped open
- [ ] Whether the building has a designated evacuation lift for disabled guests. The session's 'never use a lift' rule is for ordinary lifts; any evacuation lift is used only as the evacuation plan says.

## ⚠️ Slips, trips and falls

- [ ] Where wet floor signs, mops, spill kits and cleaning equipment are kept
- [ ] Which footwear the venue expects for each department
- [ ] Who to report a hazard to on each shift (and the venue's hazard reporting method)
- [ ] Known problem areas such as cellar steps, kitchen floors, the marquee and outdoor paths in winter

## 💪 Manual handling

- [ ] Where trolleys, sack trucks, keg lifts, hoists and other handling aids are kept
- [ ] The venue's own arrangements for moving kegs and gas, and who is allowed to do it
- [ ] Laundry arrangements (bag sizes, trolleys, stairs) and luggage trolleys
- [ ] Who to ask for help on each shift
- [ ] Any venue weight guidance, if the manager has set one

## 🩹 First aid awareness and reporting accidents

- [ ] Who the first aiders (or the appointed person) are on each shift, and how to reach them
- [ ] Where the first aid boxes are, and who restocks them
- [ ] Where the accident book is kept and who may see it
- [ ] The venue's address and how to describe the entrance for an ambulance
- [ ] Who the manager on duty is for reporting purposes and who makes RIDDOR reports
- [ ] Where defibrillator is, if the venue has one

## 🤝 Dignity at work: equality and preventing harassment

- [ ] Who staff report harassment or discrimination to (name and role), and who they go to if the complaint is about that person.
- [ ] Where the written equality, dignity at work and anti-harassment policies and the grievance procedure are kept and how staff get a copy.
- [ ] How guest or supplier harassment is logged and how a manager follows it up (for example an incident form), and what the venue does about a guest who harasses staff.
- [ ] Any external confidential support the venue offers (employee assistance line, for example), if there is one.
- [ ] GM to confirm the policy and training records satisfy the 'all reasonable steps' duty, and to re-check the date for guest and supplier harassment liability.

## 🔒 Data protection and guest privacy

- [ ] Who the data protection contact or lead is (name and role) and how to reach them out of hours.
- [ ] Which systems and messaging apps staff are allowed to use for guest and wedding details (booking system, email, any approved work messaging) and where the policy is kept.
- [ ] Where paper records, wedding files and keys are locked away, and the clear-desk or shredding arrangements.
- [ ] How a subject access request or other privacy request is logged, and who answers it.
- [ ] How a suspected breach is reported and logged (form, phone number) and who decides whether the ICO is told.
- [ ] GM to confirm the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 changes have been checked against the venue's privacy notices and request process.

## 🛡️ Safeguarding and spotting exploitation

- [ ] Who the designated safeguarding lead is (name, role, phone), who deputises, and who to go to out of hours or if the concern involves the lead.
- [ ] Where the venue's safeguarding policy is kept and how a concern is recorded (form or log).
- [ ] Venue rules on unaccompanied children, for example in the bar after a set time (these come from the premises licence and house policy, not this session).
- [ ] Whether the venue works with local police or a local safeguarding partnership, and any contact details they have given.
- [ ] GM to confirm the local police non-emergency route and who calls it after a concern is raised.

## 👁️ Security awareness and suspicious behaviour

- [ ] Who the manager on duty is and how to reach them quickly, including at weddings and events.
- [ ] The venue's evacuation and invacuation points and how staff are told, and where guests go if the building must be cleared (links to the fire safety session).
- [ ] Where lost property and the cloakroom or bag drop are, and how found items are logged.
- [ ] Which areas are staff-only and how doors and keys are controlled.
- [ ] Whether Martyn's Law applies to the venue (it depends on capacity, so the GM needs to confirm the figure used and when duties start) and who is responsible for the venue's plan.
- [ ] How staff access the free ACT Awareness e-learning and how completion is recorded.

## 🧼 Food hygiene essentials

- [ ] Where the handwash basins, sanitiser and colour-coded boards and cloths are kept, and the colour code in use
- [ ] The venue's cleaning chemicals, contact times and the cleaning schedule
- [ ] Who to tell when you are unwell (name and phone number), and the fitness-to-work return form if one is used
- [ ] The venue's pest control contractor and where pest sightings are logged
- [ ] Where delivery checks are recorded, and who can accept or reject a delivery
- [ ] The venue's own fridge limit if stricter than 8C

## 🥜 Allergen awareness

- [ ] Where the allergen matrix or allergen information is kept, and how it is kept up to date
- [ ] Who the allergen lead (or the manager responsible for allergen questions) is on each shift
- [ ] How an allergy order is flagged on the till, ticket or pass at this venue
- [ ] Where the venue's allergen-safe utensils, boards and prep area are, if it has them
- [ ] Where the nearest adrenaline auto-injector held by the venue is, if any, and who is trained to help
- [ ] The notice that tells guests to ask staff about allergens, and where it is displayed
- [ ] How pre-packed-for-direct-sale (PPDS) items, if any, are labelled here

## 🌡️ Temperature control and food safety records

- [ ] The venue's own fridge and freezer limits, if stricter than the legal 8C (for example a 5C target)
- [ ] The venue's own cooking and reheating target temperature, if it uses a higher figure than the FSA minimum
- [ ] Where the daily temperature records and the Safer Food Better Business diary are kept, and who signs them off
- [ ] Where the probe thermometers and probe wipes are kept, and how often the venue calibrates them
- [ ] Who to tell when a fridge, freezer or hot-holding unit is out of temperature, and the venue's rule for what happens to the food
- [ ] The venue's cooling method (blast chiller, ice bath or shallow trays) and where cooled food is labelled and stored
- [ ] How the venue handles hot display or hot service for weddings and events

## 🔪 Safe use of kitchen equipment, knives, burns and scalds

- [ ] Which machines (slicer, mixer, mincer and so on) the venue has, and which staff are authorised and trained to use each one
- [ ] Where each machine's guard, isolator and emergency stop are, and how to lock it off before cleaning
- [ ] The venue's knife storage (block, magnetic strip or drawer) and where cut-resistant gloves are kept, if used
- [ ] How the fryers are filtered, drained and changed, and who is allowed to do it
- [ ] Where the first aid kit, the nearest cold running water, and the first aiders are
- [ ] How to report faulty equipment and how it is tagged or taken out of use
- [ ] Where the wet floor signs and spill kit are kept

## 🍺 Dealing with drunken guests and refusing service

- [ ] Who the designated premises supervisor (DPS) is and how to reach them at any hour
- [ ] Where the incident or refusals log is kept and how to fill it in
- [ ] The premises licence conditions that apply here (for example door, capacity or last-orders rules)
- [ ] Who to call first on a shift (duty manager) and the phone numbers
- [ ] The venue's safe-ways-home options (local taxi firm numbers, rooms available, who may arrange a lift)
- [ ] The venue's rule for a refused guest who insists on driving off: who decides to call the police.

## 🪪 Age-restricted sales and Challenge 25

- [ ] The venue's age verification policy: whether it is Challenge 21 or Challenge 25, and where the written policy is displayed
- [ ] Where the refusals log is kept and how to fill it in
- [ ] Whether digital ID is accepted here (see legalNotes: this is changing)
- [ ] Whether 16 and 17 year olds may drink beer, wine or cider with a table meal here, and any premises licence conditions on children
- [ ] What to do with a suspected fake ID (who to tell, where it is kept) and who to call
- [ ] Update the written age verification policy if the venue decides to accept digital ID (registered digital verification service only); until then the physical-ID teaching applies.

## 🧯 Conflict, aggression and lone working

- [ ] Who to call first for help on each shift (duty manager) and their numbers
- [ ] The lone-working check-in procedure: who you tell, how often and what happens if you miss a check-in
- [ ] Which areas are remote or risky when working alone (cellar, car park, outbuildings, marquee) and the plan for each
- [ ] Where incidents of aggression and abuse are reported and recorded
- [ ] Late closing routine: who locks up, who leaves with whom, how staff get home safely

## 🛢️ Cellar safety: kegs, CO2 and gas

- [ ] Where the cellar gas (CO2) alarm and its warning light or sounder are located, and what the alarm sounds like here
- [ ] Who is trained and authorised to change CO2 cylinders and connect or disconnect kegs
- [ ] Where the cellar ventilation switch or fan is and how to tell it is working
- [ ] Where the safe place to gather is, and who raises the alarm and calls 999
- [ ] Where the cellar emergency procedure and the CO2 risk assessment are kept

## 🛏️ Turning and flipping mattresses and bed-making

- [ ] Which mattresses in the venue need turning or flipping, how often, and which are no-turn types (your manager will show you).
- [ ] How heavy the mattresses are and which beds have handles (your manager will show you; no weight limit is quoted in this session).
- [ ] Which beds are on raised bases or have wheels or glides, and which beds are best made as a pair (twin, king, super king).
- [ ] Where the housekeeping trolleys and linen sacks are kept, and the manual handling risk assessment for housekeeping.
- [ ] Who to report heavy or damaged mattresses to, and how pain or aches are logged (accident book or manager).
- [ ] Confirm the venue's own rule that mattresses are always turned by at least two people, and that staff will not be penalised for reporting aches or damaged equipment (the session promises this).

## 🧪 Cleaning chemicals and COSHH

- [ ] Which cleaning chemicals and products the venue actually uses in each department (your manager will show you).
- [ ] Where the safety data sheets are kept and how to get to them quickly.
- [ ] Which gloves and eye protection to wear for each product, and where the PPE is stored.
- [ ] Exactly how to dilute each product, and which dispensing systems or measures are used.
- [ ] Where chemicals are stored and who has keys, plus where eye wash and first aid kit are and who the first aiders are.
- [ ] Who to report spills, splashes and fumes to, and how they are recorded.

## 🪜 Working at height and ladders

- [ ] Which ladders, stepladders, platforms and step stools the venue has, where they are kept and who may use which (your manager will show you).
- [ ] Who is trained to use anything beyond a stepladder, such as towers or mobile platforms.
- [ ] Who is trained for marquee and event rigging, and the arrangements for contractors.
- [ ] How ladders are inspected and logged, and who to report damaged equipment to.
- [ ] Any heights where the venue has said a task must not be done by staff at all.

## 💧 Legionella and water safety awareness

- [ ] The venue's legionella risk assessment, who the responsible person is, and who carries out water checks (your manager will show you).
- [ ] The flushing schedule: which outlets, rooms and showers, how often, and how it is recorded.
- [ ] Which rooms and outlets are little used or likely to sit empty (for example seasonal or event rooms).
- [ ] The shower head and hose cleaning and descaling schedule, and who does it.
- [ ] How and to whom to report dirty, stagnant, smelly or tepid water, and what happens next.
- [ ] Where the water temperature checks and records are kept by the maintenance team.
- [ ] Who in this venue actually takes the water temperature readings and keeps the records (the session says 'usually the maintenance team').


## For Ward Hadaway or the GM to confirm before relying on the sessions
- **Sexual and other harassment by guests and suppliers:** the commencement of the "all reasonable steps" standard and employer liability for third-party harassment (reported as 30 October 2026). Check the regulations.
- **Allergen information:** whether written allergen information for every dish has become mandatory; the allergens session describes the legal minimum and the FSA's best practice.
- **Martyn's Law:** whether the venue (its largest wedding or function capacity, 200 or more) is in scope, and when duties start.
- **Age verification:** the venue's written policy against the Mandatory Licensing Conditions (Amendment) Order 2026 (digital ID is now optional through a registered provider); the Challenge 25 wording.
- **Crime and Policing Act 2026:** whether the child abuse reporting duty touches any venue activity involving children.
- **Cellar CO2:** that a CO2 risk assessment exists and says whether a gas alarm is needed.
- **Manual handling:** that the "two people to turn a mattress" rule matches the venue's assessment.
- **Data protection:** the privacy notice and the complaints route after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.
- **First aid:** who the first aiders or appointed persons are on every shift.
- **Anything a session's `legalNotes` marks as unverified.**
